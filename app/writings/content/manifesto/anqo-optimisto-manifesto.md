Hello world. This is my own version of the Techno-Optimist Manifesto, which I have dubbed the Anqo-Optimisto Manifesto. Yes, I'm a techno-optimist. I believe that most of humanity's greatest challenges – poverty, disease, environmental threats, etcetera – are solvable through human finesse and ingenuity.

A BIG asterisk here though, because I think that this optimism rests on a single, fragile assumption: that the people building the future are not only not evil, but are actively committed to the best possible outcome for each individual human. Sometimes one is not intentionally evil, but through a lack of intentional benevolence, causes inadvertent evil outcomes.

***The only antidote to this is to be intentionally benevolent.***

That conviction is the foundation that underpins the utopia that feels, to me, within reach.

## What drives me

I hope that someday, perhaps near the end of my life, I will be able to look back and say that I built something that bettered the lives of others… or at least tried my best to. Right now, I feel like I'm standing on a mountaintop at dawn, looking over the Promised Land as the first light gathers just before the sun breaks open. I've just finished my time in undergrad, at the best college in the world for me (college fit is very important!). I'm about to continue my journey at Oxford, where I hope to meet people who will change the way I think and teach me new things about Life. Yet I can't help but feel like that is an activity to complete before the dawn truly arrives. When I think about my future, I cannot picture anything other than one deeply intertwined with building something tangible from the ground.

If you ask monks why they chose a life of such asceticism, many will tell you it is a calling. And I feel, very deeply, that my calling is to build. I've tried so many different paths and careers, and every single one leads me back to the same place. I want to build. I want to learn from failures and I want to learn from successes. I'm invigorated by the constant evolution that is inherent to entrepreneurial pursuits.

And I don't mean building just to chase an exit. Or building because I romanticise the idea of being a founder. I don't want my "exit" to be selling to Meta. To have my creation to be dismembered, hollowed out, turned into sand by the winds of history.

I want my only exit to be revolutionising the way people live. To take civilisation down a new path. To build something where you can point to an exact moment in human history and say, "That is when Anqi made X."

I want to build because I can see a better world, and there is nothing more meaningful to me than spending my Time striving toward it.

## What I see for the future

As an active builder at the forefront of AI, it's near impossible not to be confronted by the sheer variance in possible outcomes as we move forward. Put simply: if we get this wrong, we are really not going to be having a good time.

I am completely and acutely aware of the potential pitfalls in the structures we are building. But I strongly believe that the distribution of outcomes is more skewed toward the positive than many people may think. For one simple reason:

***I believe in the indomitable human spirit.***

As long as humans persist, there will be striving, and as long as there is striving, we inch inevitably closer to utopia. Our tendencies towards improvement far outweigh our tendencies toward destruction.

~

I do think there is probably unsustainable hype around AI right now. But I also think the new era we're entering will fundamentally change how humans work. Put it this way: the money is fake – but the utility is real.

There will be casualties in this revolution, for sure. But I think the structures that endure will be the ones that directly solve a real human pain point. I may be wrong about a lot of this. But I think that big foundation models will not all win. There only needs to be so many large foundation models. One could argue that their big foundation model is marginally better at solving Math Olympiad problems than another foundation model. But after a certain point, those differences really dissolve. Maybe there will be 2-3 surviving large foundation models. I don't think it will ever be a big enough human pain point that there are not enough foundation models to choose from, and 2-3 big competitors is pretty good for maintaining market competition.

I also think that in some years we may come to realise we overreacted to how much compute and data we need after all. Good applications of AI do not have to be extremely compute- or data-intensive. I think that the market is somewhat blinded by the shine of huge, complex models right now, and is underestimating the value of a small but exquisitely tailored AI solution to a simple, specific problem. *If you're craving grapes, you could pay to go to a whole buffet filled with all the finest foods, and just scrounge for a grape, but perhaps a better way to solve your craving is to simply go open your fridge and eat a grape.*

All of this points to one conclusion: the real value of AI lies in how it is applied, and how humans interact with it. The good news is that there is still so much to build in productising AI and creating scalable, reliable systems that genuinely help people. And there has been no better time in history to be building. New AI tools are making it more accessible than ever for regular people to access skills, technology, and expertise that were once out of reach. Now all we have to do is ensure that we build with intentional benevolence and make the benefits of AI accessible to all.

***There is so much work for us to do, so much more for us to build.***

~ Anqi Qu
