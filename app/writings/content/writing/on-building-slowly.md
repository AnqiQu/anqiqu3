---
title: On Building Slowly
date: 2026-08-04
---

There is a particular kind of patience that building rewards. Not the patience
of waiting, but the patience of returning — coming back to the same small problem
a hundred times until it finally yields.

I used to think speed was the point. Ship it, move on, let the next thing teach
you what the last thing couldn't. And there is truth in that. But some things
only reveal themselves slowly, to people who are willing to sit with the same
questions long enough to hear them differently.

## What slowness buys you

- **Depth.** You notice the second and third layer of a problem, the parts that
  don't announce themselves.
- **Taste.** You start to feel the difference between a solution that works and
  one that's *right*.
- **Ownership.** Slow work is legible to you later. You know why every piece is
  where it is.

> The best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago. The second best time is
> now.

None of this is an argument against urgency. It's an argument for choosing,
deliberately, which things deserve your slowness — and then giving it to them
without apology.
